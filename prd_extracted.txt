1   Executive Summary

The BEMS FutureSkills Accelerator is BEMS Institute's main new training program, following Technuture 5.0. Its first group of students starts in October 2026 across four courses: AI & Automation, Web Development, Product Design (UI/UX), and Cybersecurity. Classes run both in person at the Umuahia labs and live online, reaching secondary-school leavers, MOUAU students, NYSC members, and people learning from elsewhere.

The Accelerator is designed to do more than teach: it signs up learners, trains them until they're ready for work, helps the best of them get hired by BEMS and partner companies, and then uses those success stories to attract the next group of students. The first group isn't a one-off — it's the start of a cycle that keeps feeding itself.

This document lays out the full case for it — how the money works, the journey a student takes from advert to job, what makes BEMS hard to copy, and the tools to run it all as it grows — so leadership can approve it with the numbers in front of them.

4.2  Finishing is everything — so we build for it

A student who drops out costs us money, gives us no success story, and can't be helped into a job. So keeping students until they finish is the most important thing the program does. Here's how:


A welcome session before class starts: a live “here's why you'll finish” session. Anyone who doesn't show gets a personal WhatsApp message.


A public goal: each student states their goal in the class group. Saying it out loud, in front of others, makes people far more likely to follow through.


Small projects along the way, not one big exam: students build a small real project every 2–3 weeks, so they can see themselves making progress and keep their momentum.


Automatic reminders: miss two classes and the system sends an automatic check-in message (see Section 6).


A second chance: a student who falls behind can rejoin those weeks in the next group for free, once. That turns a drop-out into a finisher instead of a refund.




5   Why BEMS Is Hard to Copy

Any competitor can copy a course outline in a day. What they can't copy quickly is a trusted local name that actually gets students into jobs. BEMS's edge comes from four things, and each one gets stronger every time we run another group of students.


5.1  Four things competitors can't easily match


We stand behind the result. A clear “get job-ready or keep learning with us for free” promise means BEMS is judged on whether students succeed, not just on running a class. Free courses and most local rivals won't make that promise.


We're here, and we're known. Real labs in Umuahia, a recognised institute name, a presence at the LGA and MOUAU, and tutors students can meet in person. An online-only or out-of-town provider simply can't match this in Abia.


We can hire our own graduates. BEMS is a technology group spanning fintech, govtech, and business software. Being able to employ the best graduates — and introduce others to partner companies — is an advantage no stand-alone training school has.


Every group makes the next one easier. Each group of students produces graduates, success stories, and results that make the next group cheaper and easier to fill. That builds up over time; a copycat has to start from zero.


5.2  The cycle that feeds itself

This is the heart of the whole plan: run the first group of students not as a one-off, but as the thing that fills the next group.

HOW EACH GROUP FEEDS THE NEXT

Sign up students  →  train them until they're job-ready  →  get the best ones hired (BEMS + partners)  →  show off those real success stories  →  and those stories sign up the next group more cheaply.

Each time around, it costs less to attract students and they'll pay a bit more. By the third group, most new students come from word of mouth and employer demand — the cheapest and most trusted way to grow.


5.3  Word-of-mouth built in from day one


Bring a friend: any student whose friend signs up and pays gets ₦5,000 in credit or airtime. That turns 80 students into 80 people spreading the word.


Graduates come back to teach: the best graduates return as paid teaching assistants — affordable extra help, strong role models, and a reason for them to stay connected.


A “where they are now” page: a public, regularly updated page showing where our graduates work — the single most convincing piece of marketing the program can have.

Re-typing every sign-up, sending every reminder one by one, chasing every payment individually is how a small team burns out and students slip through the cracks. BEMS will build these systems in-house. 6.2  Three automatic processes worth setting up first


New sign-up → welcome: a new sign-up is added to the list automatically, gets a welcome message and payment details, and is booked for a welcome call. This tackles the people who pay but never start.


Paid → enrolled → reminded: once payment is confirmed, the student is marked “enrolled,” added to the class group, and their attendance starts being tracked. Miss two classes and they get an automatic check-in. This tackles drop-outs.


Finished → help into a job: finishing the final project adds the graduate to the job-help list and the graduate group. This closes the loop.

The plan — a sign-up page, fliers, QR banners at three locations, handbills, and messages about job prospects — fits an Umuahia audience well. Three things make it work: talk about the result (a job) rather than the features, keep track of which advert brings students so money goes where it works, and once the first group produces a success story, put that story front and centre.

WHERE WE ADVERTISE — AND HOW WE TRACK IT


QR banners (BEMS Hub, LGA Secretariat, MOUAU): each banner's QR code has its own link, so we can see which location brings the most sign-ups and move a banner that isn't working.


Fliers and a short video: focused on the result — a job — and the option to pay in parts, aimed at people in Umuahia and around MOUAU.


Handbills at campus spots and town centres: printed with a QR code, not just a phone number, so people who pick one up can sign up on the spot and we can count them.


Graduate success stories (from the first group on): the “where they are now” page and short testimonials become the main way we attract the next group — the self-feeding cycle at work.
